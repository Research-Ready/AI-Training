# AI Training Product Redesign Context

## Purpose of this document

This document captures the reasoning, decisions, product direction, and redesign plan for the AI training repository.

It is meant to preserve context so we can return to this later and understand:

- why the training is being redesigned
- what the first product should become
- what should be kept from the existing material
- what should be removed from the first version
- how the repository should be restructured
- how the product should serve the end-user
- how this connects to future products

The main point is simple:

The current training material is valuable, but it should not be treated as the product directly.

The product should be designed around the end-user.

## Core decision

The first commercial AI training product should become:

# Practical AI Literacy for Teams

## Internal working title

AI Fundamentals Without Hype

## Subtitle

AI fundamentals without hype, jargon, or risky guesswork

## Core product promise

A team enters confused about AI.

A team leaves with:

- a shared language
- basic practical understanding
- safe-use boundaries
- a first AI working agreement
- a clear next step

## Why this redesign is needed

The current AI training material contains many strong ideas, but it risks becoming too broad for a first product.

The existing material touches on:

- AI basics
- prompting
- research workflows
- source curation
- workflow engineering
- system design
- AI tools
- critical thinking
- risks
- templates
- learning journeys

This is valuable, but too much for a first 4-hour product.

The end-user does not need to see everything we know.

The end-user needs a clear, useful, bounded experience.

The first product should answer the question:

How can our team understand AI well enough to use it safely, practically, and responsibly in daily work?

## Important design principle

Do not build the product around the existing course material.

Build the product around the end-user problem.

The existing course material is a toolbox.

The product is the selected, simplified, polished learning experience that solves a specific problem for a specific audience.

## End-user problem

Many teams know AI is important, but they do not know how to start.

Common problems:

- people experiment with AI individually
- teams have no shared language
- some people are enthusiastic while others are anxious
- people do not know what information is safe to enter into AI tools
- people do not know when AI output can be trusted
- managers want AI adoption but do not know what practical first step to take
- researchers and educators worry about quality, sources, authorship, and integrity
- teams lack basic agreements about responsible AI use

This product should help with that first step.

## What the product is

Practical AI Literacy for Teams is a practical training for mixed teams that need to understand AI without becoming technical specialists.

It helps participants:

- understand what AI is in plain language
- understand what generative AI can and cannot do
- recognize why AI output can be convincing but wrong
- identify useful, risky, and unsuitable AI use cases
- discuss privacy, hallucinations, bias, and accountability
- create a first team-level AI-use agreement
- choose one concrete next step

## What the product is not

This product is not:

- a prompt engineering course
- a generic AI inspiration session
- a technical machine learning course
- a full AI implementation project
- a legal compliance audit
- a vendor-specific tool training
- a local AI installation workshop
- a RAG or automation workshop
- a system design workshop
- an advanced research AI course
- an ongoing support service
- a tool troubleshooting session

## Why this is the right first product

This is the best first AI product because it is:

- easy to understand
- easy to explain online
- relevant to current market demand
- possible to deliver in 4 hours
- useful for mixed groups
- not dependent on fragile technical setup
- compatible with existing material
- strong as an entry-level offer
- a gateway to deeper future products

It is also safer than starting with local AI, automation, or advanced workflow design.

Those are interesting future products, but they create more risk:

- too much technical setup
- more chance of things failing live
- higher support expectations
- harder sales explanation
- more custom work

This first product should be bounded, repeatable, and easy to pilot.

## End-user experience

The participant should not feel like they are attending a technical AI lecture.

They should feel:

- this is understandable
- this is practical
- this connects to my work
- I can ask questions without feeling stupid
- I now know where AI is useful
- I now know where AI is risky
- I know what our team should agree on first

The desired emotional movement is:

From:

We know AI matters, but we do not really know what to do with it.

To:

We understand the basics, we see realistic use cases, we know the risks, and we have a first team agreement.

## The main output

The training should not end with only awareness.

It should end with one or more concrete artifacts.

Preferred outputs:

- AI use-case map
- responsible AI checklist
- safe-use boundaries
- first team AI working agreement
- list of next actions

The most important output is:

A first team AI working agreement.

## Suggested product wording

Practical AI Literacy for Teams is a 4-hour hands-on training for teams that want to understand AI, use it responsibly, and create clear first agreements for daily work.

The output is a practical team artifact: a use-case map, a responsible-use checklist, and a first AI working agreement.

## Suggested format

Main version:

Physical

Possible delivery:

Remote

Hybrid:

Only with a co-facilitator and proper setup

## Why physical is preferred

Physical delivery works best because:

- trust is easier to build
- people ask questions more naturally
- post-it exercises work well
- group energy is easier to manage
- participants stay more engaged
- the facilitator can read the room

## Remote version

Remote is possible if:

- the online board or shared document is prepared
- links are sent in advance
- exercises are shorter
- instructions are very clear
- participants are actively included
- there are frequent check-ins

## Hybrid version

Hybrid is possible but risky.

Only offer hybrid if:

- the room has good audio
- remote participants can clearly hear and speak
- everyone uses the same digital board
- remote participants have active roles
- there is a co-facilitator

Without this, hybrid should not be offered.

## Core exercises

## Exercise 1: AI Myth or Reality

Goal:

Help participants separate hype from reality.

Examples:

- AI always tells the truth
- AI can help draft text
- AI understands meaning like a human
- AI can produce biased output
- AI can replace all expert judgement
- AI output must be checked

Output:

Shared list of myths and realities.

## Exercise 2: Useful, Risky, Unsuitable

Goal:

Help participants judge AI use cases.

Participants sort tasks into:

| Category | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Useful | AI can support this safely with normal checking |
| Risky | AI might help, but rules and checks are needed |
| Unsuitable | AI should probably not be used here |

Example tasks:

- summarize public meeting notes
- draft a first version of an email
- process sensitive student data
- write a final legal decision
- brainstorm workshop ideas
- summarize confidential HR information
- check grammar
- generate policy text without review
- create a research summary with sources

Output:

AI use-case map.

## Exercise 3: Better Prompt, Better Check

Goal:

Teach basic prompting and verification without becoming a prompt engineering course.

Simple prompt pattern:

- context
- task
- constraints
- desired output
- check or critique

Output:

One improved prompt and one verification step.

## Exercise 4: Team AI Agreement

Goal:

Turn learning into team practice.

Questions:

- What may we use AI for?
- What should we not use AI for?
- What must always be checked?
- What information should never be entered?
- Who remains responsible for final output?
- What do we test next?

Output:

First team AI working agreement.
